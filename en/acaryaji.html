Ācārya Sthaneshwar Timalsina

Principal custodian of the Sarvāmnāya lineage outside Nepal, scholar and founder of Vimarsha Foundation.

Ācārya Sthaneshwar Timalsina brings together two worlds that rarely meet, the traditional transmission from teacher to disciple and university scholarship, and both shape a body of work that studies Indian philosophy through its texts as well as its practice.

He holds the Nirmal K. and Augustina Mattoo Endowed Chair in Classical Indic Humanities in the Department of Asian and Asian American Studies at Stony Brook University (New York). In 2019 he founded Vimarsha Foundation, conceived as a global gurukula for the study of India’s philosophical and contemplative systems.

Traditional training

He was raised in his father’s Vedic gurukula in Nepal. Over fourteen years he received from Svāmī Premchetan Brahmacārī the complete transmission of the Sarvāmnāya, and he studied the work of Abhinavagupta with teachers in Varanasi linked to Gopīnāth Kavirāj. In 1996 he was consecrated as ācārya (ācārya abhiṣeka) and recognized as Śaivācārya of the Nepali Sarvāmnāya order. The complete genealogy of this lineage is available on our blog.

Academic career

He completed a master’s degree at Sampurnananda Sanskrit University in Varanasi (1991) and taught for several years at Nepal Sanskrit University in Kathmandu, where he launched a department devoted to the study of the Āgama traditions. While writing his doctoral dissertation, he taught at the University of California, Santa Barbara, and at Washington University in St. Louis.

He earned his doctorate in 2005 at Martin Luther University Halle-Wittenberg (Germany) with a study of the history of Advaita Vedānta. That same year he joined San Diego State University, where he taught for nearly two decades in the Departments for the Study of Religions and of Philosophy, before taking up his current chair at Stony Brook.

Research

His research spans Advaita Vedānta and consciousness studies, the Śaiva-Śākta philosophies of the Kaula and Krama streams, Indian theories of mind (recognition, memory, imagination and emotion), contemplative visualization and visual culture, yoga studies and comparative philosophy. He is the author of four monographs and more than eighty articles and book chapters, and he also writes original works in Sanskrit.
